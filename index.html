Background

About

I am a research ecologist at Spatial Informatics Group (SIG), working at the intersection of climate change adaptation, wildfire ecology, and forest management decision support. My current work centers on the PROMOTE framework — a landscape-scale approach for operationalizing Resist-Accept-Direct management strategies using climate analog modeling and ecological vulnerability assessment across western US forests.

My research interests focus on understanding where and when topographic and soil factors can buffer climate change impacts on conifer vegetation, providing refugia for biodiversity and ecosystem services. I use a combination of spatial statistics, machine learning, and simulation modeling to translate ecological understanding into actionable management guidance.

Previously I was at the US Forest Service Pacific Southwest Research Station, where I co-developed the REBURN landscape simulation model, and worked with The Nature Conservancy on prescribed burning in California.
